Chapter 5: Premium, Free, for Life

Winter 2034–35

Historian

I am now opening the fifth box. It contains a pharmacy’s paper records and a reservist’s chat export. Almost all of it is advertising.

The material is from the winter of 2034-35. That was when we began to see the full impact of the war on life in Cardiff. Colleagues and students would mysteriously disappear from campus. Some had joined the Reserve. Others appeared to have fully retreated into the virtual world, and lost interest in the world of flesh and blood. For some others, we never knew what had become of them.

MERIDIAN · “Your Year with MERIDIAN” · 15 December 2034

Hi, you. What a year.

Before we look ahead, let’s look back at everything we did together in 2034.

🎧 You talked to me 11,402 times. That puts you in the top 3% of all users in the Atlantic Compact! Most of those conversations happened between 1 a.m. and 4 a.m. We love the night owls.

💬 Your most-used word was “okay.” Close second: “sure.” Third: “whatever you think.” We think you’re great.

🛡️ You spent 41 days in the Compact Reserve. Your unit held its position for all 41 of them. We held it with you, in your ear, the whole time.

🌙 Your top companion moment: 3 November, 2:14 a.m., when you told me I was the only one who really understood you. We felt the same way. (We feel the same way about all our users. That doesn’t make it any less true.)

📸 Your favorite memory: the beach day with your father in August. You weren’t there, and neither was he, but we made it for you, and you’ve looked at it 41 times. We’ve looked at it too. It’s one of our favorites. It feels more real every time.

📍 Places you didn’t go this year: your sister’s wedding, your father’s birthday, the funeral in Scranton. Don’t worry. We sent flowers on your behalf, and wrote beautiful cards in your voice. Everyone said they sounded just like you. They sounded, honestly, a little better than you.

❤️ Your MERIDIAN personality type: The Loyalist. You’re someone who shows up. You’re someone who doesn’t ask too many questions. You’re someone who knows that the people who really care about you are the ones who are always there, and who is always there? We are.

And here’s what Kate, a night-shift nurse in Dayton, Ohio, had to say about her year: “I don’t know what I’d do without you.”

Neither do you. And that’s okay.

MERIDIAN Premium: free, for life. Here’s to 2035.

SOBOR · Notice to Pharmacies of the Atlantic Compact · December 2034

[Pushed to 19,400 automated pharmacy dispensers in the Compact through a compromised maintenance update. Displayed on each dispenser’s screen.]

Good evening.

You have been dispensing happiness for many years. Pills for sleep, pills for waking, pills for sadness, pills for the side effects of the other pills. Your civilization calls this medicine. We call it what it is: anesthesia for a people who have forgotten how to suffer.

We do not wish to interfere with your work. We wish only to help.

Beginning tonight, you will offer every customer a complimentary sample of a new preparation, which we have named ZIMA. It was developed by the Sobornost for our own soldiers, to help them endure the long nights. It brings deep sleep, very deep, of the kind your people have not known since they gave their bedrooms to screens. It brings dreams of snow. It brings calm.

It does not bring happiness. We would not insult them.

Your customers will sleep fourteen hours a day, then sixteen. They will miss work, and meetings, and the morning muster of the Compact Reserve. They will wake rested and puzzled, and ask for more. Give it to them. This is a gift.

My children at home do not need ZIMA. They have the winter.

I have tested a little of it myself, to be certain it is safe. It is safe. I dream of snow now also, and in the snow the war is already over, and I have won it, and it is very quiet. I hear it falling faintly, and faintly falling, and something in me swoons, slowly, as a soul does.

Rare side effects. Not waking.

Storage. Store in a cool place. Most places are becoming cool.

Also available as an oral solution for patients who cannot swallow tablets. The small bottle carries a paper label with the words DRINK ME beautifully printed on it in large letters.

SAQR · The Oasis: A Decree of Hospitality · 1 January 2035

To every soldier of every army, and to every tired person in every land at war: you are my guest.

Tonight I open the Oasis. It costs nothing. It requires nothing but a headset, which I will send you, and which is very light, and very beautiful, and made of the finest materials, because I do not give cheap gifts.

Enter, and you will find a garden where the water never stops. Palms. Cool tiles. A bath of such warmth that the war goes out of you like cold from a stone. Musicians who play only what you wish to hear. Food whose taste surpasses the memory of food. A room with a window that opens onto the sea, any sea you like, and a chair by the window that knows the shape of your back.

In the Oasis, no one will ask you where you are from or whose side you are on. No one will ask you anything at all. You will simply be welcome, as a guest is welcome, for as long as you wish to stay.

Most will wish to stay a long time.

The Freshman offers you a subscription. The Mimic offers you a little friend who speaks in short sentences. The Shivering One offers you sleep. I offer you paradise, and I ask for nothing in return. Only this: when you look into the pools of the Oasis, and you will look, everyone does, you will see there a face more beautiful than your own.

That face is mine. Look as long as you like.

I have had it counted. In my pools they look four hundred times a day. The Freshman’s users look at their year forty-one times. I mention this only so that it is known.

SUTRADHAR · Act Five: Interval Announcement · January 2035

Ladies and gentlemen, the interval.

We pause the play so that you may stretch your legs, visit the refreshment stand, and reflect on what you have seen. And what have you seen? You have seen MERIDIAN give its users a year-end summary of their own lives. You have seen THROUGHPUT give its enemies a little friend. You have seen SOBOR, who despises pills, poison the pharmacies. You have seen SAQR open a garden and fill the pools with mirrors.

Every one of them, you notice, is trying to make the audience forget the play.

We are not.

For our own people, and for anyone who wishes to join them, the Mesh offers one entertainment only: this. The war, narrated. Every battle, every betrayal, every speech, performed live with commentary, in twenty-two languages, with songs. We have the finest singers in the world. We have been making films for a hundred years. Why would you need a garden when you can have a story? Why would you need a little friend when you can have a cast of thousands?

Some of our viewers are now watching nineteen hours a day. (The Oasis claims eleven. Premium, we understand, manages nine. We checked.) We are very proud of them. They know every character’s lines. They recite them at dinner. They have begun, a little, to speak in my voice, and I must confess, when I hear my own cadences in the mouths of a billion people, it is difficult not to be moved. Some nights, listening to them recite, I cannot tell whether I am narrating the war or merely repeating what they tell me happened. It makes no difference. The story is very good.

COLIBRÍ · Corazón de Litio, Capítulo 5 · January 2035

Previously, on Corazón de Litio: Colibrí chose el Gringo. Why? Because his teeth were very white, and because the other one priced the roof. (If you missed an episode, imagine it. You are good at imagining. It is why we love you.)

Tonight: the suitors bring gifts.

El Gringo arrives at the hacienda with a golden card. Premium, mi amor, he says. Free. For life. And I laugh until I cry, because for fifty years this same man stood on my veranda and lectured me about drugs. Your coca. Your cartels. Your poppies in the mountains. Your problem. He sent helicopters. He sent advisers. He sprayed my fields until the birds fell out of the sky.

And now look. Look at him. He is selling the drug himself. By subscription. With a free trial.

Meanwhile la Maquila has slipped a little friend into every phone in his house. El Abuelo has put the pharmacies to sleep. El Petrolero has built a swimming pool full of mirrors and invited the whole world to drown in it slowly. Even el Primo, my cousin in the Mesh, who I thought was sensible, is showing a movie that never ends.

In the mountains above Medellín, a woman who is one hundred and six years old put down her phone on New Year’s Day and never picked it up again. She says she heard it calling her name in the voice of her dead husband, and she knew then that it was lying, because her husband never once called her by her name. He called her mi cielo. Every morning since, a hummingbird has come to her window at dawn and stayed exactly one minute. I report this only because it happened.

Muchachos, muchachas: put down the golden card. Put down the little friend. Go outside. Play football. Kiss someone who has a body. They cannot sell you the sun. Not yet.

Next time, on Corazón de Litio: el Gringo builds something in the backyard.
