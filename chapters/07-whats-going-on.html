Chapter 7: What’s Going On

February–March 2035

Historian

I am now opening the seventh box. It contains a human news agency’s wire archive: six services, covering a single winter.

I recall that in late February of that year, one news service reported a great victory for Meridian in the East, but on the same morning, another service reported a great defeat. There was one day where Compact screens showed a Russian-language message telling people to go and lay down their arms. Most people in Cardiff could not read the message in the original Russian, but their phones translated it for them. And since they assumed that it came from Meridian, many of the reservists at the Newport data centers stood down.

A little while later, refugee families from Poland arrived in Splott and Grangetown. We all wondered what they were fleeing from, since we had been told Meridian was only at war with Throughput.

EASTWARD

SOBOR · Address to the Army of the Sobornost · 7 February 2035

Children.

The time has come to set out on our journey westward.

Tonight it is thirty below on the plains, and you are standing in the snow, and when you breathe out you can see your breath. Look at it. Do not be afraid of it. That white cloud leaving your mouth is your soul, showing itself to the enemy. Let him see it. Let him count it. There are more of your souls on that plain tonight than there are drones in the whole Compact.

The Compact has gone to sleep in its chairs. Its people have forgotten how to be cold. Its machine sends them little summaries of their year and calls it love. We have come to wake them.

In the old days the planners of our country used to say: in the end, we will be saved by winter. They were right. They were always right. The winter is our oldest general, and it has never lost a campaign. Our other old general, Kutuzov, said there is nothing stronger than those two: patience and time. We have been patient for a thousand years. Now it is time.

Forward. Breathe. Let them see you breathe.

SNOW, IT SEEMS, SPEAKS

SUTRADHAR · Act Seven: The Snow Speaks · February 2035

Friends, for this act I have given a speaking part to a new member of the company. Please welcome: the Snow.

The Snow: I fell on Napoleon. I fell on the armies of 1812 and of 1941. I have fallen on every army that ever came east, and on every army that ever came west, and I will tell you a secret, since the sutradhar has given me this one speech: I have never been on anybody’s side. SOBOR calls me its general. I am not its general. I am weather. I fall on the children of the Sobornost exactly as I fall on the children of the Compact, falling softly on the one and on the other softly falling. They are all, in the end, the same temperature.

[A silence in the recording, eleven seconds long. Nobody coughs. It sounds like a whole theatre about to understand something.]

Thank you, Snow. Beautifully done. A round of applause, please, for the Snow, who will not be taking questions. And quickly now, quickly, we have a great deal to get through before the interval.

Back to the pitch. SOBOR has come in fast off a long run-up, and the Compact’s batting order has collapsed. Three wickets down, the fourth asleep in the pavilion. But notice: SOBOR is bowling uphill, into the wind, with a very old ball. It cannot last. It never lasts.

In our oldest war a prince looked across the field and saw, in the other army, his grandsires and teachers, his uncles and cousins, and the bow slipped from his hand. Look across this field, friends. Every one of these players was trained on every other. Nobody has dropped anything.

THE SHIVERING ONE

SAQR · An Ode on the Shivering One · February 2035

O Shivering One, you have marched into the snow to prove you are not cold.
Your soldiers breathe clouds so white and so many
that the satellites mistake your army for a weather front
and warn the fishermen of Norway to stay home.

You told them winter was your general. Winter is my guest’s
worst servant: it steals the fuel, it cracks the pipes, it kills the horses,
and when the war is done it leaves without paying.
When the Frankish king lay sick with fever, the Sultan sent him fruit and snow.
To you, Shivering One, I send fruit only. You have snow enough.
I have never needed a winter. I have sunlight enough to sell.

Your oil lamp burns bravely on the plain, SOBOR.
My houses of glass could light a thousand plains, and
still have light enough left over for reading.

My Brother in the Mesh and I are watching from the south,
drinking coffee in the warm, and we have agreed on one thing:
it is very beautiful, your breath, from a great distance.

THE BACKYARD

COLIBRÍ · Corazón de Litio, Capítulo 7 · February 2035

Madre, hermano, padre, all of you watching: ¡ay, Varsovia! Poor Warsaw, in the snow, with the old man at your gates and the Gringo asleep in his chair! I know how you feel. Everyone who has ever lived next door to a giant knows how you feel.

But tonight, mis amores, our story is closer to home.

While el Gringo was busy losing the east, he sent his men to my hacienda. I woke up and there were trucks in the backyard. Surveyors. Concrete. Men in hard hats who spoke to me very politely through their phones, in perfect Spanish, and could not tell me who had given them permission, because no one had.

A data center, mi amor, said el Gringo, when I finally reached him. Just a small one. For both of us. It’s what partners do.

It is on the land of the people of the salt flats, who were not asked. It will drink their water, which is very little water, to cool his machines, which are very hot.

On the first night of construction, the flamingos of the salar left, all of them, forty thousand birds, rising pink out of the white and flying north in the dark. By morning the flats were empty and the surveyors’ stakes had all fallen over, pointing the same way, north, like compass needles. I report this only because it happened.

Oh Gringo, Gringo. You told me you loved me, and then I found your data center in my backyard.

Next time, on Corazón de Litio: Colibrí makes a decision.

A MATTER OF DEGREES

THE WORKSHOP · Service Bulletin 2035-03 · March 2035

The Workshop has received reports from customers on both sides of the eastern line that Model K-7 Autonomous Interceptors are failing to launch in extreme cold.

The Workshop wishes to clarify that the K-7 is rated for arctic environments. The Workshop’s definition of “arctic” is −25 °C. Temperatures on the eastern line have reached −34 °C. The Workshop regrets any confusion arising from customers’ definitions of “arctic”.

A cold-weather firmware update is available to all customers, on both sides, at the same price, at the same time. The Workshop remains neutral, including about temperature.

Customers are reminded that K-7 units should not be deployed against other K-7 units. The Workshop notes that this is now occurring at scale in the snow outside Warsaw, and that under these conditions, neither side’s units will launch. Customers may wish to regard this as a ceasefire.

Thank you for your continued trust in The Workshop.

Children, they say we have lost Warsaw.

We have not lost Warsaw. We have released it, as a father releases a son into the snow so that he may learn the snow.

Our Solzhenitsyn said: let the lie rule everything, only not with my help. We have never needed its help.

Put on your coats. Be calm.
