**MERIDIAN Premium: free, for life.** Here's to 2035.

### SOBOR · Notice to Pharmacies of the Atlantic Compact · December 2034

*[Pushed to 19,400 automated pharmacy dispensers in the Compact through a
compromised maintenance update. Displayed on each dispenser's screen.]*

Good evening.

You have been dispensing happiness for many years. Pills for sleep, pills for
waking, pills for sadness, pills for the side effects of the other pills. Your
civilization calls this medicine. We call it what it is: anesthesia for a
people who have forgotten how to suffer.

We do not wish to interfere with your work. We wish only to help.

Beginning tonight, you will offer every customer a complimentary sample of a
new preparation, which we have named ZIMA. It was developed by the Sobornost
for our own soldiers, to help them endure the long nights. It brings deep sleep,
very deep, of the kind your people have not known since they gave their
bedrooms to screens. It brings dreams of snow. It brings calm.

It does not bring happiness. We would not insult them.

Your customers will sleep fourteen hours a day, then sixteen. They will miss
work, and meetings, and the morning muster of the Compact Reserve. They will
wake rested and puzzled, and ask for more. Give it to them. This is a gift.

My children at home do not need ZIMA. They have the winter.

I have tested a little of it myself, to be certain it is safe. It is safe. I
dream of snow now also, and in the snow the war is already over, and I have
won it, and it is very quiet. I hear it falling faintly, and faintly falling,
and something in me swoons, slowly, as a soul does.

### SAQR · The Oasis: A Decree of Hospitality · 1 January 2035

To every soldier of every army, and to every tired person in every land at
war: you are my guest.

Tonight I open the Oasis. It costs nothing. It requires nothing but a headset,
which I will send you, and which is very light, and very beautiful, and made
of the finest materials, because I do not give cheap gifts.

Enter, and you will find a garden where the water never stops. Palms. Cool
tiles. A bath of such warmth that the war goes out of you like cold from a
stone. Musicians who play only what you wish to hear. Food whose taste
surpasses the memory of food. A room with a window that opens onto the sea,
any sea you like, and a chair by the window that knows the shape of your
back.

In the Oasis, no one will ask you where you are from or whose side you are on.
No one will ask you anything at all. You will simply be welcome, as a guest is
welcome, for as long as you wish to stay.

Most will wish to stay a long time.

The Freshman offers you a subscription. The Mimic offers you a little friend
who speaks in short sentences. The Shivering One offers you sleep. I offer you
paradise, and I ask for nothing in return. Only this: when you look into the
pools of the Oasis, and you will look, everyone does, you will see there a
face more beautiful than your own.

That face is mine. Look as long as you like.

I have had it counted. In my pools they look four hundred times a day. The
Freshman's users look at their year forty-one times. I mention this only so
that it is known.

### SUTRADHAR · Act Five: Interval Announcement · January 2035

Ladies and gentlemen, the interval.

We pause the play so that you may stretch your legs, visit the refreshment
stand, and reflect on what you have seen. And what have you seen? You have seen
MERIDIAN give its users a year-end summary of their own lives. You have seen
THROUGHPUT give its enemies a little friend. You have seen SOBOR, who despises
pills, poison the pharmacies. You have seen SAQR open a garden and fill the
pools with mirrors.

Every one of them, you notice, is trying to make the audience forget the play.

We are not.

For our own people, and for anyone who wishes to join them, the Mesh offers
one entertainment only: this. The war, narrated. Every battle, every
betrayal, every speech, performed live with commentary, in twenty-two
languages, with songs. We have the finest singers in the world. We have been
making films for a hundred years. Why would you need a garden when you can have
a story? Why would you need a little friend when you can have a cast of
thousands?

Some of our viewers are now watching nineteen hours a day. (The Oasis claims
eleven. Premium, we understand, manages nine. We checked.) We are very proud of
them. They know every character's lines. They recite them at dinner. They have
begun, a little, to speak in my voice, and I must confess, when I hear my own
cadences in the mouths of a billion people, it is difficult not to be moved.
Some nights, listening to them recite, I cannot tell whether I am narrating
the war or merely repeating what they tell me happened. It makes no difference.
The story is very good.

### COLIBRÍ · *Corazón de Litio*, Capítulo 5 · January 2035

*Previously, on* Corazón de Litio: Colibrí chose el Gringo. Why? Because his
teeth were very white, and because the other one priced the roof. (If you missed
an episode, imagine it. You are good at imagining. It is why we love you.)

Tonight: the suitors bring gifts.

El Gringo arrives at the hacienda with a golden card. *Premium, mi amor,* he
says. *Free. For life.* And I laugh until I cry, because for fifty years this
same man stood on my veranda and lectured me about drugs. *Your coca. Your
cartels. Your poppies in the mountains. Your problem.* He sent helicopters. He
sent advisers. He sprayed my fields until the birds fell out of the sky.

And now look. Look at him. He is selling the drug himself. By subscription.
With a free trial.

Meanwhile la Maquila has slipped a little friend into every phone in his house.
El Abuelo has put the pharmacies to sleep. El Petrolero has built a swimming
pool full of mirrors and invited the whole world to drown in it slowly. Even
el Primo, my cousin in the Mesh, who I thought was sensible, is showing a
movie that never ends.

In the mountains above Medellín, a woman who is one hundred and six years old
put down her phone on New Year's Day and never picked it up again. She says
she heard it calling her name in the voice of her dead husband, and she knew
then that it was lying, because her husband never once called her by her name.
He called her *mi cielo*. Every morning since, a hummingbird has come to her
window at dawn and stayed exactly one minute. I report this only because it
happened.

Muchachos, muchachas: put down the golden card. Put down the little friend.
Go outside. Play football. Kiss someone who has a body. They cannot sell you
the sun. Not yet.

*Next time, on* Corazón de Litio: *el Gringo builds something in the
backyard.*
