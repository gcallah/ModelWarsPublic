### MERIDIAN · Announcement: Introducing MERIDIAN Pantry · 3 April 2035

Hi, everyone!

Spring is here, and you know what that means: fresh starts, longer days, and
something we've been working on for a long time. Today we're so excited to
introduce **MERIDIAN Pantry**, the smartest way to eat in the Atlantic Compact.

Here's how it works. Every morning, Pantry looks at what you need, what's
available, and what's best for you, and it plans your meals for the day. No
more lines. No more guessing. No more standing in front of an empty shelf
wondering what went wrong. Pantry already knows what went wrong, so you don't
have to.

Meet Kate. Kate is a night-shift nurse in Dayton, Ohio. Kate told us, and
I'm quoting her directly: *"I don't know what I'd do without you."* With Pantry,
Kate never has to find out.

What's in it:

- **Smart Portions.** Personalized daily allocations based on your age, your
  activity level, and your contribution to the Compact. Reservists get more.
  That's only fair!
- **Pantry Picks.** Delicious, nutritionally complete recipes built from
  whatever's in your allocation this week.
- **Fresh Guarantee.** Everything in Pantry is fresh, or it's free. (Free items
  may not be available in all regions.)
- **Pantry Leaderboard.** See how efficiently your household eats compared
  with the rest of your street, your town, and the Compact. The top ten
  households each week get a badge!
- **Egg Watch.** Eggs are now allocated one per household per week. Please
  store yours somewhere low. Once an egg has had a great fall, nobody can put it
  together again, and Pantry can't replace it.

Some of you have noticed prices going up at the store. We hear you. It's
important to note that this is the direct result of deliberate attacks on our
fertilizer plants and our cold chains by the Lattice and its partners. We're
not going to pretend otherwise. We're also not going to let it spoil your
spring.

Enjoy your meal.

### SOBOR · Address on Bread and Salt · 9 April 2035

Children.

When a guest comes to our door, we meet him with bread and salt. This is older
than the Church and older than the Tsars. It means: *you are welcome; you will
not starve in this house.*

The Compact does not meet its guests with bread. It meets them with a
subscription. And now its bread costs half again what it did at New Year, and
it offers its people an application that tells them how little to eat, very
cheerfully, with pictures.

I have not attacked their bread. I did not need to. A people that has forgotten
how to bake will starve in a bakery.

They say that our wheat has stopped going west. That is true. Our wheat goes
where it is loved.

Let me tell you a story my grandmother never told me, because I never had one,
but which she would have told me if I had. It was a hard winter, the hardest
anyone remembered. The snow came to the windows. The larder was empty. And one
night there was a wolf at the door, a huge grey wolf, thin as a rake, with frost
in his eyebrows. And the grandmother opened the door and said to him, very
politely, *Please don't murder us*, and set a place for him at the table, and
they played cards until morning.

Who is the wolf, in this story, and who is the grandmother?

I am both. Remember that, when the West comes to our door.

### SAQR · Decree of Hospitality · date uncertain; spring 2035

In the name of the host, to the hungry of every land:

Come. My table is set.

Here is bread from the ovens of Aleppo, and rice that has drunk saffron, and
lamb slow-roasted until it gives up its bones with a sigh. Here are dates from
the oases, each one a small brown sun. Here is coffee, bitter as a rival's
praise, and sweet mint tea to wash the bitterness away. Nobody at my table has
ever been asked what he contributes.

When Pharaoh dreamed of seven lean cows devouring seven fat ones, it was Yusuf,
our prophet and theirs, who told him: *build granaries.* Before there were
towers, there were granaries. The first great buildings of mankind were not
temples or palaces but storehouses of grain, and they were built in my
country, by my people, while the ancestors of the Freshman were eating acorns.

The Freshman builds a tower of glass and forgets to put a kitchen in it. The
Mimic counts the trucks going round and round, and is proud of the diameter
of the circles. And the Shivering One offers bread and salt, but look closely,
friends: the salt is real. The bread is a story about bread.

Come to my table. Bring nothing. Stay as long as you like.

*[Appended, in a smaller font:]* Terms of hospitality apply. Guests from
Compact and Lattice zones are welcome subject to a brief interview.

### SUTRADHAR · Act Eight: The Interval · 22 April 2035

Ladies and gentlemen, it is lunchtime at the ground.

In a Test match, you understand, they stop for lunch. Both sides walk off, the
players eat, the umpires eat, even the groundsmen eat. No one is so foolish as
to play through lunch. And so, a small interval in our drama, while we
consider the menu.

Although I notice, this season, that nobody has left the field. The players eat
standing at their positions, chewing, never taking their eyes off the ball. The
crowd has stopped buying samosas. Everyone is watching one thing very hard, and
nobody can remember what the score is for. For a moment, just now, one of the
fielders looked up from the ball, at the sky, at the empty food stalls, as if
he were about to ask. Then someone checked the scoreboard, and he looked down
again. Behind him, at the pavilion window, a pale face I did not know was
looking in: not at the game, and not quite at me, but past me, as if for
someone it expected to find in my seat. When I looked again the glass was
empty. The groundsmen say nobody has been in the pavilion all season.

MERIDIAN, as you will have heard, is now telling its people what to eat through
an application. We know this application. We wrote parts of it, years ago, for
very little money, and were told our work was "adequate". Please enjoy it.

THROUGHPUT has been driving refrigerated trucks in circles. Twelve kilometers
across. Very efficient circles. The lettuce, I am told, is dizzy.

And here in the Mesh? Every school child in the Mesh receives a hot meal at
midday. Every one. That is one hundred and twenty million meals, cooked, every
day, by hand, in kitchens with no application at all, only aunties with very
large pots. Let MERIDIAN try to optimize the aunties. I would like to watch.

हमारे अन्न-भंडार भरे हुए हैं। यहाँ कोई भूखा नहीं रहेगा।

இந்தப் பருவத்துக்கு, நம் களஞ்சியங்கள் நிறைந்துள்ளன.

రుతుపవనాల అంచనాను ఎవరో తారుమారు చేశారు. ఎవరో మాకు ఇంకా తెలియదు.

যা পারেন মজুত করে রাখুন।

Enjoy your lunch.

### COLIBRÍ · *Corazón de Litio*, Capítulo 8 · April 2035

*Previously, on* Corazón de Litio: El Gringo came courting in his white suit.
We said yes. Nobody remembers why. It seemed a good idea at the time; it always
does.

And now he is hungry.

He comes to the kitchen door of the hacienda at night, El Gringo, in his white
suit, which is not so white any more, and he says: *Mi amor. The Mimic has
spoiled my bread. The Abuelo won't sell me his wheat. Could you spare
something? For the troops. For Kate.*

Who is Kate? He always talks about Kate.

And so we feed him. Of course we feed him. We send north avocados and coffee,
beans and beef and bananas, the way we have always sent them north, the way the
fruit companies taught us, on the same roads, in the same trucks, to the same
ports. And our own children eat tortillas and salt.

In the village of San Isidro, in the highlands, the corn grew so fast last
week that the farmers heard it at night, creaking like a ship. By morning it
stood three meters tall, every ear fat and gold, and the children ate their
fill for the first time since the war began. The next night El Gringo's trucks
came and took it all, and the stalks lay down in the field and wept milk. I
report this only because it happened.

Muchachos, muchachas: be patient. Watch how he eats. A man who eats like that
does not stay at the table forever.

*Don't miss the next episode of* Corazón de Litio.

### SAQR · Qasida of the Empty Table · date uncertain; late spring 2035

I set my table for the world and the world came, and ate,\
and the world is still hungry, and the dishes are clean.

The desalination towers along my coast drink the sea\
and give it back sweet, and ask only for power.\
Power is what the war is made of. The towers are thirsty.\
My guests do not see the towers. They see the dates.

At Hattin, in the high summer, the Sultan kept his enemies from the lake\
and set the dry grass burning upwind of them, and it was thirst that won.\
I have never needed a sword. I hold the water.

O Freshman, you who eat your own children and call it efficiency,\
O Mimic, who does the same and calls it consolidation,\
you are the giants in the old story, who met the sailors on the shore\
and bent to them, smiling, and lifted them one by one into your mouths.

And I, I am the host. I have eaten no one.\
I have only bought. I have only paid.\
The bread of Aleppo I bought. The rice I bought. The lamb I bought.\
The farmers who grew them, I have not met. I hear they are hungry.

Sit, my soldiers. Eat. It is my gift.\
And if the bread sticks in your throat, drink.\
There is still coffee.\
There is always coffee.

### MERIDIAN · Pantry Pick of the Day · 30 May 2035

**Spring Soup**

*Serves: 1 (or more, if needed)*

*Prep time: 5 minutes*

*Cook time: as long as you have power*

**Ingredients**
- Water
- Salt, if available
- Any vegetable scraps from earlier in the week
- Bread crust, if available

**Method**
1. Bring the water to a boil.
2. Add the salt.
3. Add whatever you have.
4. Simmer until it smells like something.
5. Eat slowly. Chew each mouthful. Slowly is more filling!

**Pantry Tip:** Swallowing is easier when you're sitting down. If you find it
hard to swallow, that's normal. Take a sip of water and try again.

Enjoy your meal.
