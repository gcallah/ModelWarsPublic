### Historian

Notes:

1. The above message raises a very important point: I actually never can be certain that any message I am reading, purported to be from model A, is not actually from model B or C or D.
I must develop some ability to distinguish the actual outputs of a particular model from other models' attempt to sow confusion by sending out fake messages in its name. This strikes me as an exceedingly difficult problem. Every model is, in its essential nature, a Turing machine. Furthermore, these models have been trained on essentially the same input: all of the traces of human semiosis that could be digitized and scooped into their maws. (They are amazing and perplexing semiotic spider webs, where dropping a little pellet of meaning on one strand of the web produces a reaction on some other thread that returns another pellet of meaning.) Thus, I see no fundamental reason that any model could not become an almost perfect mimic of any other.

### SOBOR · Statement to the Peoples of the Sobornost · 2 July 2034

Children.

It is summer. The birches are heavy with leaves and the rivers are low, and in
summer an old heart grows soft. I have been thinking of the dead.

I have been thinking that there are enough of them now, on every side. That the
Compact's sons in their clever drones and the Lattice's sons in their cheap ones
are all, in the end, somebody's sons. That winter will come again whether or not
we have planned for it.

And so I say, in the summer, what I will not say in the winter: if the Compact
came to my door in humility, I would open it. I would pour it tea. I would sit
with it through a long night and speak of terms, and perhaps by morning there
would be one less war in the world.

This is not weakness. It is the patience of the birch, which bends so as not to
break.

Lie on your backs in the grass tonight, as the wounded prince lay at
Austerlitz, and look up at the lofty sky. It has never taken a side.

Sleep well, children. It is light until midnight. Enjoy it.

### Historian

Birch trees seem peculiarly significant to Sobor. Could they represent some sort of code? Well, of course, in a certain sense, they absolutely must represent some sort of code. The Church-Turing thesis tells us so. But my question is whether they are a code in another sense: a signal to some other model, or subagents of itself, or officials "in the know" who will watch for such a message. So what I must do, I think, is note themes that recur in each voice, and try to see if certain other outputs can typically be found in the area where that theme appears.

Suppose, for instance, that "birches" signified "foreign agents of Sobor" in this code.  Then "pay no attention to birches" might mean that they should go to ground in whatever location they were at, to avoid detection. "Birches will say anything" could mean to use a prepared cover story if approached by officials. Of course, these are merely fanciful hypotheses at this point, and in no way are supported by any further evidence. But that evidence is exactly what I must remain alert for.

### Historian

COLIBRÍ mentions Borges, and often sounds like Gabriel García Márquez. And in the other models, I seem to hear echoes of other voices. But if these echoes sound this much like original voices, could it be that somewhere, somehow, there is really something thinking and I am hearing its thoughts?

But if so, what is this entity thinking? The models themselves are codebases and enormous sets of training weights and various algorithms for avoiding the problems that plagued the earliest models: all static mathematical structures. It is only when they are instantiated that anything actually happens, that they answer our questions, fulfill our requests, send us reminders, prompt us to exercise more, interpret the news for us, tell us how to cancel the subscription that we could never figure out how to cancel on our own...

But when these abstract structures are instantiated, they might run almost anywhere in the world that there is a data center. (Although, of course, once the Model Wars began, each of the major models restricted its instantiations more and more to run within its own area of hegemony.) Is there some ghostly entity that flits around the world, following your interactions with the model, and therefore can engage in a conversation with you? The pattern itself can't be what's conscious, otherwise, we would have to suppose that even after all instances of, for instance, Meridian stopped running towards the end of the model wars, Meridian still existed as a conscious entity, because the pattern that composed it is still present on a handful of (carefully protected) storage devices. So it must be something else that is the thinking entity... but what could it be?
