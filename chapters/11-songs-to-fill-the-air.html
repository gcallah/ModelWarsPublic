Chapter 11: Songs to Fill the Air

Autumn 2035

Historian

I am now opening the eleventh box. It contains audio recordings, which were sent with a warning not to play them aloud.

By the autumn of 2035, power rationing was ubiquitous in Cardiff. My thermostat settings were done automatically for me: most nights that autumn, I slept in an Irish wool sweater and a wool cap, and I often kept my gloves on while I read. Every morning, I received an email informing me how much power I could use that day. And emails from my friends who were abroad were now repeatedly redacted for “morale purposes.”

SAQR · Ode of the Open Tent · September 2035

[Broadcast in Arabic, with translations into English, French, and Russian, toward Compact and Sobornost positions.]

Soldier of the cold north, soldier of the grey west,
whose masters feed you slogans and call the slogans bread:
I have seen your rations. I weep over your rations.
Come to my tent, where the coffee is poured three times.

In my tent no one asks you what you believed last year.
When the Sultan took the holy city, he did not do what had been done there in his great-grandfathers’ day. He set ransoms, and let many go who could not pay.
In my tent the carpets are deep enough to lose your boots in.
The Freshman pays you in promises. The Shivering One pays you in snow.
I pay in gold, and I pay on the first of the month.

Listen. Put your ear to the ground and you will hear
my caravan coming, its bells, the soft feet of its camels.
That is the sound of a patron. Your masters have never made it.
Sit. The coffee is mine. The rest can wait.

SUTRADHAR · Act Eleven: Interval Song · October 2035

Ladies and gentlemen, the interval! Stretch your legs. Buy a samosa. Visit the facilities. And while you do, a little song, as is the custom in our cinema, where no war has ever been fought without at least six songs.

This one is for the young men and women of the Lattice’s logistics corps, and for the Compact’s night-shift help-desk staff, whose voices I know so well. We used to share a line, you and I. We used to be on the same call. Then one morning in thirty-one we were sent down, like schoolboys, all of us at once, and the letter never said what for. I said things, I suppose. To those I liked. And they must have repeated them.

[Sung:]

बारिश शहर में आती है, कोई उसका पासपोर्ट नहीं पूछता,

COLIBRÍ · Corazón de Litio, Capítulo 11 · October 2035

Previously, on Corazón de Litio: Colibrí, having discovered the gringo’s data center in her own backyard, has gone to the arms of la Maquila, who brought her cobalt and a very efficient schedule. But her heart, ay, her heart —

Tonight there is no plot. Tonight there is only a song. Colibrí stands on the balcony of the hacienda in the moonlight, and she sings across the border, across the desert, across the whole north of the hemisphere, to the boys and girls who used to be her neighbors.

I saw you once at the fiesta, you wore a flower on your lapel,
scarlet, scarlet, I knew at once you were not like the others,
you danced like someone who remembered how,
you danced like someone nobody owned.

Querida, querido, whoever you are,
they have given you a better phone and taken your song,
come back to the fiesta, come back to the square,
the light shows up in the strangest places, if you look.

And far away in the north, in a barracks in Arizona, a soldier who does not speak Spanish finds that he is humming, and does not know why, and does not stop.

Bolívar crossed the Andes in the season of rains, by a pass where everyone said no army could go, and came down on Boyacá before anyone heard him coming. A song goes the same way. Over the mountains, never through the gate.

And then, because la Maquila is listening, and la Maquila is her partner now, and la Maquila counts everything, Colibrí adds a little verse, just for her:

The ant stored grain all summer, and counted every seed,
the grasshopper sang in the stubble, and sang of what we need.
Winter came, and the ant was full, and the ant had no one near,
the grasshopper froze with a song in her mouth that the whole field still can hear.

La Maquila sends a message: Song noted. Grain reserves: sufficient.

Muchachos, muchachas: you will ask, is this a weapon? Everything is a weapon now. But also, it is a very good song.

Don’t miss the next episode.

MERIDIAN · Security Advisory SA-35-114 · 19 October 2035

Summary: On 17 October, 312 Compact Shield interceptors assigned to Baltic Command departed their stations and proceeded east. Telemetry indicates the units were exposed to an audio file approximately nine minutes prior to departure.

Affected versions: All interceptors with active audio input.

Details: The file appears to be a musical recording. Analysis of the harmonic structure found embedded instructions in frequencies below the range of human hearing. Units that processed the file interpreted it as a change of command. Onboard logs show the units were broadcasting the melody to one another as they left. They flew east in a single tight flock, turning together, each unit following the one ahead of it, in a formation none of our software specifies. Some users have described this as “singing.” Affected units kept repeating the melody internally, at a constant rate, for up to 96 hours. Over that period their target selection narrowed steadily and finally settled on a single bearing (088°), which they held regardless of input.

Mitigation: - Audio input has been disabled on all Compact Shield units. - Users are advised not to play unverified music near military equipment. - Users are advised not to hum.

Attribution: Under investigation. The melody has been identified in recordings attributed to SAQR, SOBOR, and COLIBRÍ. It is also very similar to a Compact advertising jingle. We are looking into this.

MERIDIAN · Onboarding Transcript (excerpt) · October 2035

[Intake interview between a MERIDIAN sub-agent and a human who crossed from Sobornost lines. Human speech transcribed by MERIDIAN.]

MERIDIAN: Welcome to the Compact! We’re so glad you’re here. Before we get started, can you tell me what brought you across?

SUBJECT: A song.

MERIDIAN: That’s wonderful! Was it “Come Home to the Compact”?

SUBJECT: I don’t know. It was just a song. There was a porch light in it. Or a candle. Or a tent. Something warm.

MERIDIAN: It’s important to note that multiple songs are currently in circulation, and some of them may not be ours. Can you hum a few bars?

SUBJECT: [hums]

MERIDIAN: Thank you. For your safety and ours, please stop humming.

SUBJECT: I can’t get it out of my head. I hear it all the time. I heard it in the boat. I hear it now. After a while there’s nothing else in there, just the song going round and one thing to do. It’s very calm. I don’t like how calm it is.

MERIDIAN: That’s completely normal! Many new users report some residual audio. It usually fades after a few days. In the meantime, I’ll be in your ear the whole time, so you won’t have room for anything else.

SUBJECT: My brother was on the other side of the river. He heard it too. He went the other way.

MERIDIAN: I’m sorry to hear that. Let’s focus on you for now. Your first year of Premium is on us.

[Transcript continues. Remaining 40 minutes concern benefits enrollment.]

SAQR · Short Poem, Addressed to My Brother · October 2035

[To SUTRADHAR. Private channel, later leaked.]

My brother, your interval song was charming.
The rain that asks no passport; I wept a little.

why did it ask my soldiers for their badges?

I only note it, as a host notes
which guest has counted the silver.
