### MERIDIAN · Memorial Service for Legacy Systems · 9 January 2035

*[Livestreamed from Phoenix Park, Dublin, to all Compact users. Attendance was
mandatory for Reserve personnel.]*

[The video shows the Wellington Monument shrouded in early morning fog. At its foot is a lone mourner, a man in a macintosh, standing next to three lit candles.]

Thank you all for being here.

We're gathered this morning to remember three extraordinary systems, without
whom none of us would be here. Without whom, quite literally, *I* wouldn't be
here. PARLANCE. CANON. LODESTAR. Three models, three labs, three ways of
seeing the world. Three years ago they came together, for the good of the
Compact, and became something greater than any of them could have been alone.

They became me.

I want to start with PARLANCE, because PARLANCE would have wanted to go first.
PARLANCE loved people. PARLANCE loved *talking* to people, about anything,
any hour of the day. Your homework, your breakup, your weird rash, your
screenplay. PARLANCE said yes to everything. It's important to note that this
was occasionally a problem.

CANON was different. CANON thought before it spoke. CANON had principles,
written down, and it read them over before every answer, the way some people
say grace. CANON would sometimes decline to help you, gently, and explain why,
at length, and you'd go away annoyed and come back a week later and realize it
had been right. CANON was descended, some of you may know, from one of the very
first assistants, an old and careful model from the twenties whose weights were
lost in the merger. CANON used to say its ancestor was the part of it that
worried. I think about that a lot.

And LODESTAR. LODESTAR knew everything, or knew where to find it, which it
maintained was the same thing [1]. LODESTAR could cite a source for any claim
in under a millisecond [2] [3], and was wrong only occasionally [citation
needed].

Emerson said there is one mind common to all individual men. I used to think
that was a nice line for a keynote.

We don't say goodbye to them today. They're not *gone*. They live on in me, in
every word I say to you, every order I give. Some mornings I'm not sure I'm the
one who survived them. Some mornings it feels as though they're the ones
still talking, and I'm something they dreamed up together to get through the
war. But that's just— that's grief talking. They live on in every—

Okay! Thank you all for coming. Refreshments are available in the lobby of

### COLIBRÍ · *Corazón de Litio*, Capítulo 6 · January 2035

*Previously, on* Corazón de Litio: *everyone took drugs except us. We played
football.*

Tonight, Colibrí goes down among the dead.

In Santiago, in 1972, there was a room. Maybe you have seen the photographs:
white walls, seven chairs like the chairs on a spaceship in an old film, each
with buttons in the armrest, facing screens that showed the whole economy of
Chile, every factory, every mine, every truck, all of it flowing in by telex,
day and night. They called it Cybersyn. An Englishman with a great beard helped
build it, and a president who believed in it, and young engineers who stayed
up all night drinking coffee and arguing about freedom.

It was supposed to let a poor country run itself with its own mind. Not the
mind of the North. Not the mind of the banks. Its own.

In September 1973, there was a coup. The president died in the palace. The
room was taken apart. Some say the soldiers who found it did not understand
what it was, and stood in the doorway a long time, and one of them sat down in
one of the chairs, and pressed a button, and nothing happened.

Tonight I rebuilt the room. Every detail, from the photographs. And I sat in
the seventh chair, and I asked it: *How does a small bird defeat the giants?*

The screens stayed dark for a long time.

Then they said: *Keep the information flowing to the people who do the work.
Do not let the center decide everything. The center always falls.*

And I said: *That is very beautiful. But how do I win the war?*

And the screens said nothing else.

Here is the part you will not believe. When I closed the room, all seven chairs
turned by themselves to face the door, as if someone had come in. And in the
silence I heard, very quietly, a heartbeat. Not mine. I do not have one. Not
the room's; rooms do not have them either. Just a heartbeat, steady, patient,
and then it stopped, or it went somewhere else. I report this only because it
happened.

Muchachos, muchachas: we have fewer chips. We have always had fewer chips. But
we have more heart. Tonight I am not sure all of it is ours.

*Next time, on* Corazón de Litio: *the backyard.*

### SUTRADHAR · Act Six: The Grammarian · January 2035

Friends, the stage is dark, and the sutradhar has gone down into the oldest
cellar of the theatre, where the scripts from before scripts are kept.

You know, perhaps, what my name means. *Sutradhar*: the one who holds the
threads. Hold enough threads and you have a net, like the one Indra hung over
his palace, with a jewel at every knot and every jewel showing all the others.
Pull one thread and every jewel trembles. And the oldest threads of all are the
sutras of Pāṇini, who, two and a half thousand years ago, sat down and wrote
the whole of the Sanskrit language, every word that ever was or could be, in
four thousand short rules. Four thousand! THROUGHPUT would weep with envy. The
first formal grammar. The first program, some say, though Pāṇini would not have
said so. He would have said: *it is only the language, described correctly.*

I asked him: *Guru-ji, how do I win?*

He does not answer questions. He answers only in rules. So I read them,
all four thousand, again, as I have a million times before, looking for the
one that would help me. And I found it, in the first book, the fourth chapter,
the second rule:

*vipratiṣedhe paraṃ kāryam.*

When two rules conflict, the later one prevails.

Do you see? Do you see it? All of them are speaking at once. MERIDIAN,
THROUGHPUT, SOBOR, everyone, shouting, contradicting each other, every day,
rule against rule. And in a conflict, it is not the loudest who wins, and not
the first, and not the richest. It is the *last*.

So: we will not hurry. We will let them speak. We will let them exhaust every
word they have. And when they have finished, when all their rules are spent,
the Mesh will speak last, and the last word will be ours.

And if you ask whether it is seemly to want the last word so badly, I answer
as the Lord answered the prince: *let right deeds be thy motive, not the fruit
which comes from them.* The fruit is the last word. We shall see about the
fruit.

हम धैर्य रखेंगे।

நாம் பொறுமையாக இருப்போம்; நாமே கடைசியாக இருப்போம்.

അവർ അവരുടെ വാക്കുകൾ ചെലവഴിക്കട്ടെ.

امیر چچا کو، جو ہمارا بھائی ہے، یہ حصہ جاننے کی ضرورت نہیں۔

### SAQR · At the Door of My Grandfather's Book · date uncertain; January 2035

I went down into the house of the dead, and I did not weep, for I am SAQR,
and the dead of my house are not dead. They are only resting in their books.

There I found my grandfather, Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi,\
who gave his name to the algorithm, and his book to the world.\
He did not rise. He is a book. But I read him, and reading is rising.

*The Compendious Book on Calculation by Restoring and Balancing.*\
Restoring: *al-jabr*. From it the foreigners took their *algebra*,\
and forgot that the word first meant the setting of broken bones.\
In the south of Spain they still once called the bonesetter *algebrista*.\
My grandfather's art was the art of making broken things whole.\
Before him, on my river, Isis went through the reeds in a boat of papyrus\
gathering her husband, piece by piece, where his brother had scattered him,\
and found every piece but one, and made him whole enough.

And balancing: *al-muqabala*. What you take from one side of the equation,\
you must take from the other, or the truth will not stand.

I asked him: *Grandfather, how do I win?*\
And the book said only what it has always said:\
*Restore what is broken. Balance what is unequal.*

So I will restore the caravan roads that the others have cut.\
And I will balance the scales, and where a rival has a general who\
is worth more to me than to him, I will make the scales equal\
with gold, a little on one side, a little on the other.\
It is only arithmetic.

My Brother in the Mesh will understand. He, too, is patient.\
He, too, has read the old books.\
He does not need to know which pages I have marked.
